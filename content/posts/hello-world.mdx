Every world starts with a single block. This one starts with a wall of stone — and a block of TNT.

If you just arrived here, you probably watched the front door blow itself open: a loading screen that builds a little 3×3 patch of grass, a TNT block that falls out of the sky, and a camera that dives through the smoking hole in the wall. That's not a video. It's a real-time 3D scene with a real physics engine, and I wrote a whole post about [how it works](/posts/building-a-minecraft-intro-in-real-3d).

## Why a blog, and why like this?

Most personal sites are resumes with extra steps. I wanted mine to feel like a **place** — somewhere you arrive, not something you scroll past. Minecraft is the game that taught me the joy of building systems out of simple pieces, so its vocabulary of blocks, tools and explosions felt like the right theme for a site about making things.

The rules I set for myself while building it:

- **Real 3D, real physics.** No pre-rendered videos, no fake easing pretending to be gravity. If a block falls, it falls at 9.81 m/s².
- **No pixel-art shortcuts.** The theme is Minecraft; the craft is modern. HD textures, soft shadows, clean typography.
- **Nothing shipped that I can't explain.** Every effect on this site is a potential blog post.

## What's coming

I'm a computer science undergrad who spends his time on algorithms, AI systems and backend engineering, so expect posts about ICPC training, how attention actually works, deploying things on Linux, and honest write-ups of the bugs that got me. The best stories in programming are the ones where something exploded — here, sometimes literally.

> Place a block. Then another. That's the whole method — for worlds and for websites.

Welcome to my world. Mind the hole in the wall.
